Getting Started & Staying on Task
Task initiation is a growth area for Leo. These tools build the habit of starting quickly and staying focused through a 3-hour shift with no break.

Note: Leo's shifts are 3 hours with NO break. Use this timer to practice focusing in timed blocks. Build up from 10 minutes to 30 minutes over your first few weeks.
Task: Sorting items from a bin
05:00
💡
Leo's rule: If you notice you reached for your phone during the timer — put it down and return to the task. Don't judge yourself. Just come back. That IS the skill.
1
The 3-Second Launch Rule
When given a task, count 1–2–3 in your head — then move your hands toward it. Don't think about the whole task. Just do the first physical action.
"My hands go to the bin. I pick up the first item. That's my only thought."
2
Write the Steps First
If a task feels overwhelming, write the steps on a notepad before you start. This is how engineers and contractors think before they build.
Step 1: Open bin. Step 2: Sort by category. Step 3: Place in pile. Step 4: Report done.
3
No Break = Plan Your Energy
Leo's shifts are 3 hours with no break. Eat before your shift. Drink water. Wear comfortable shoes. Physical comfort helps mental focus — especially for 3 solid hours.
Eat → arrive → clock in → 3 hours → clock out → rest. That's the whole shift.
4
Ask for a Step-by-Step Walkthrough
Leo works best when instructions are clear and in order. It is always okay to ask. "Can you walk me through it?" is professional, not a problem.
"Before I start, can you walk me through the steps? I want to make sure I understand."
5
Use Your Body — Movement Helps
Leo is a physical learner. If you feel stuck, physically walking to the task and touching it re-starts your focus. Your body knows what to do.
"I'm going to walk to the bin right now." The movement starts everything.
⭐
Leo's superpower: Once he understands a task and gets going, he is diligent and does not give up. Employers value this enormously.

Why it matters: Eye contact at work signals "I'm listening." You don't need to be good at it — you need one simple strategy.
🎯 The Triangle Zone Strategy
1
Look at the nose or forehead
From the other person's view, looking at their nose or forehead looks exactly the same as eye contact. Try it in a mirror — it works.
2
2–3 seconds, then look away naturally
Look for 2–3 seconds, glance away, look back. This is exactly what most people do in conversation.
3
Nod while listening
A small nod shows you're engaged even when not making direct eye contact. Combined with the nose/forehead trick, this looks completely natural.
📱
Practice idea: Try the nose/forehead trick while watching YouTube videos. Look at the presenter's nose instead of their eyes. It gets natural within a week.

These are rules most workplaces never say out loud — but they matter. Leo can save or print this page.
1
Greet coworkers when you arrive
A brief "hey" or "good morning" is enough. It signals you're friendly and present.
"Morning." or "Hey, what's up." (a nod also works)
2
No headphones means no headphones
This is a real workplace rule — full details in Module 4. Even one earbud can be a problem when your supervisor calls you.
3
If a coworker seems upset — it's probably not about you
Workplaces have moods. If someone is short with you, they're likely having their own hard day. Keep doing your job.
4
Being quiet is fine — being responsive is required
When a supervisor or coworker speaks to you directly, responding promptly matters. Even "got it" or a nod is enough.
Quiet + responsive = respected employee.
5
Mistakes happen — report them immediately
If you break something or do something wrong, telling your supervisor right away shows integrity. Not telling them is much worse than the mistake.
"I made a mistake with [thing]. I wanted to tell you right away. What should I do?"
6
Always close out your shift with your supervisor
Before leaving, find your supervisor and let them know you're done. Don't just disappear when your time is up.
"I finished [last task]. I'm heading out — see you next time."

Phone & Headphone Rules at Work
No judgment — just clear rules, real reasons, and strategies that work. This module protects Leo's job.
⚠️
Real talk: Phone use and headphones at work are the #1 reason young workers lose their first job. Leo deserves to know the real stakes so he can protect this opportunity.
📋 What supervisors see vs. what they think
❌ Supervisor sees Leo on his phone
Leo has headphones in.
Leo isn't responding when called.
Leo is looking at his phone instead of working.
What the supervisor thinks
"He doesn't care about this job."
"He's not taking this seriously."
"I can't rely on him."
✅
What they want to see: Phone in pocket, head up, responsive when spoken to, focused on the task. That's the whole game.
🔊 Why headphones are especially a problem at Goodwill
📦
Safety: In the back-of-house, people are moving carts, lifting bins, working in tight spaces. If you can't hear what's around you, it creates a safety risk for everyone.
🗣️
Communication: Your supervisor may give instructions from across the room. Headphones in with music loud = you won't hear them. Missing instructions means doing the wrong task.
👥
Team signal: Headphones signal "I'm closed off." Supervisors and coworkers hesitate to approach someone with both ears covered. This isolation can hurt Leo's reputation at work.
📌
Leo's shifts: 2–3 times per week, 3 hours each shift, NO break or lunch. This means there is NO designated "okay time" for phone use during the shift.
✗
NO headphones during your shift — at all
This means the entire 3-hour shift. Whether sorting in back, walking to a different area, or waiting for the next task. Headphones off means fully off, not "just one ear."
Exception only if your supervisor specifically permits it for a specific situation.
✗
NO video games on your phone — ever during a shift
Leo's 3-hour shifts have no break. There is no "free time" during work hours. Video games are for after the shift ends, full stop.
Shift ends → clock out → games. Not before.
✗
NO scrolling social media while on shift
Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Snapchat — all off during your shift. Even 2 minutes of visible scrolling sends the wrong message to your supervisor.
✓
Phone stays in pocket on SILENT
Silent, in your pocket, untouched. It's with you the whole time — you can use it the moment your shift ends. Having it nearby gives security without becoming a distraction.
Clock in → phone on silent → phone in pocket → do not touch until shift ends.
✓
Emergency calls — tell your supervisor first
If a family member might need to reach you urgently, tell your supervisor at the start of your shift. This is respectful and responsible.
"My parent might need to reach me today. Is it okay if I step out briefly to take a call if needed?"

🧠
Real strategies for real urges. Leo loves music and his phone. These strategies work with that reality, not against it.
1
Make a deal with yourself before the shift starts
Before you walk in, say out loud: "My shift is 3 hours. I'll listen to my music the moment I'm done." This makes the wait feel shorter because there's a clear endpoint.
"3 hours. Then music. As loud as I want. I earned it."
2
Replace the urge with a physical action
When you feel the pull to reach for your phone, immediately move your hands to the task. The physical action of grabbing an item replaces the urge. This works with practice.
Feel phone urge → hands go to task → urge fades in about 30 seconds.
3
Use the Focus Timer to practice at home first
Before your first shift, use the Module 2 timer to practice 15–20 minutes without touching your phone. It builds the mental habit before you need it at work.
Practice at home → work feels familiar. Home = training. Goodwill = the real thing.
4
Make it a personal competition
Every shift, try to beat your own record. "Last shift I went the whole 3 hours." Track it mentally. You're competing with yourself, not anyone else.
Shift 1: try. Shift 2: try again. Shift 3: streak. Keep going.
5
The post-shift music reward
When your shift ends and you clock out — headphones in, volume up, music on. You earned it completely. Make this feel like a real reward, not just a habit.
Clock out → headphones → walk to car/bus with your music at full volume. That's the routine.
🎵
Leo loves music — that's real and valid. The goal is not to take music away. It's to protect the job that builds toward a career. 3 hours without music is a small price for that future.
