Code of Conduct & Respect at Work
Every California employer covers these basics in new-hire orientation. This is how Goodwill expects everyone to treat each other.
⚖️ Treating everyone equally (non-discrimination)
👥
The big idea: Every employee must be treated fairly no matter their race, color, religion, sex, age, national origin, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity, or veteran status. This is the law in California, not just a company preference.
✅
What this looks like day to day: Treating every coworker and customer the same way — with basic respect — regardless of who they are or how they're different from you.
🔒 Confidentiality
1
Don't share personal information
If you see a donor's or customer's personal information (like on a receipt, ID, or form), it stays private. Don't share it with anyone, including friends or on social media.
2
Don't post about work on social media without permission
Photos of the store, coworkers, donors, or anything work-related should not be posted online unless you're specifically told it's okay.
⏰ Attendance & punctuality
⏱️
Be on time, every time. Arrive a few minutes early so you're ready to start exactly at your shift time. If you're going to be late or absent, call your supervisor as early as possible — don't just not show up.
👔 Dress code & ID badge
🪪
Wear your ID badge at all times during your shift. Follow whatever dress code Goodwill gives you (closed-toe shoes are very common in donation/sorting areas for safety). Ask your supervisor if you're unsure what to wear.
🚫 What NOT to do at work
✗Bring weapons, alcohol, or illegal drugs to work
✗Use offensive language, slurs, or jokes about someone's identity
✗Take store merchandise or donations without paying/permission
✗Use your phone for games, videos, or social media during your shift (covered in Part 1)
✗Leave your station without telling your supervisor

Preventing Sexual Harassment
California law requires every employer to teach this to every employee. This is what the video will almost certainly cover.
📜
Why this exists: California state law requires all employers with 5+ employees to give every employee training on this topic, so that everyone knows what's okay, what's not okay, and what to do if something happens. It's required for ALL new hires, in every job, everywhere in California — not specific to Goodwill.
❓ What counts as sexual harassment
1
Unwelcome comments or jokes
Comments about someone's body, sexual jokes, or comments about someone's gender or sexual orientation that make a person uncomfortable.
2
Unwanted physical contact
Touching, patting, hugging, or any physical contact that the other person doesn't want — even if it seems "friendly" to the person doing it.
3
Showing inappropriate images or messages
Sharing sexual pictures, videos, or messages at work — on a phone, computer, or in person.
4
Asking someone out repeatedly after they said no
Continuing to ask a coworker for a date, their number, or to "hang out" after they've already said no.
🌍 Important facts to remember
It can happen to anyone. Any gender can experience it, and it can come from a coworker, supervisor, or even a customer.
It doesn't have to be "on purpose." If a behavior makes someone uncomfortable, it's still a problem — even if the person doing it thought it was funny or harmless.
It is never the victim's fault. No one "asks for it" by what they wear, say, or how they act.
🗣️ What to do if it happens (to you or someone else)
1
Tell your supervisor or manager right away
You don't have to handle it yourself. Reporting it is the correct, professional response — every time.
"I need to talk to you about something that happened. Someone made me uncomfortable and I think it should be reported."
2
You will not get in trouble for reporting
This is called "retaliation," and it's against the law. Reporting a problem is the right thing to do — it can't be used against you.
3
There are people outside Goodwill too
The California Civil Rights Department (CRD) handles these issues for the whole state, if needed: 800-884-1684.

Workplace Safety Basics
Sorting donations involves boxes, carts, and sometimes sharp or heavy items. These are the basics every retail/warehouse worker learns.
🏋️ Lifting safely
1
Bend your knees, not your back
Squat down to pick up heavy items, keep the item close to your body, and stand up using your legs. Never bend over at the waist to lift something heavy.
2
If it's too heavy — ask for help
Asking a coworker to help lift something heavy is normal and expected. It is not a sign of weakness — it's the safe and correct choice.
"This is heavy — can you help me lift it?"
🧤 Handling donations safely
1
Watch for sharp objects
Donations sometimes contain hidden sharp items — broken glass, needles, knives. If you feel or see something sharp, stop and tell your supervisor. Don't reach blindly into bags or boxes.
2
Wear gloves if provided
If Goodwill gives you gloves for sorting, wear them. They protect your hands from sharp or dirty items.
🚧 Keeping the workspace safe
1
Clean up spills immediately
A spill on the floor is a slip-and-fall hazard. If you see one, either clean it up or tell your supervisor immediately so it can be handled.
2
Keep aisles and exits clear
Boxes, carts, and bins should never block walkways, fire exits, or fire extinguishers.
3
Only use equipment you've been trained on
If you haven't been shown how to use a piece of equipment (like a baler, box cutter, or pallet jack), don't use it. Ask your supervisor to show you first.
🚨 If you get hurt — even a little
🩹
Report every injury — even small ones. A small cut or scrape might seem like "no big deal," but it needs to be reported right away so it can be treated and documented. This protects Leo, not just the company.
"I got a small cut on my hand. I wanted to let you know right away."
🔥 Emergency basics
🚪
Know where the exits are. On your first day, take a moment to notice where the nearest exit is from your work area. If there's a fire alarm or evacuation, follow your supervisor and other staff calmly to the exit — don't use elevators, and don't stop to grab belongings.

Test Day Tips for Leo
Strategies for the day of the video and quiz — when Leo has to do it on his own.
1
Watch the videos like you're going to be quizzed — because you are
When something feels important in the video (a rule, a "what to do" step, a number), pay extra attention. If you're allowed to take notes, jot down key words.
2
Read every question slowly — twice if needed
Test questions sometimes use words like "NOT" or "EXCEPT" in capital letters. These flip the meaning of the question. Read carefully before choosing an answer.
"Which of the following is NOT an example of..." — this is asking for the one that's different.
3
If you don't know — pick the most careful, respectful, "tell someone" answer
On these kinds of quizzes, the correct answer is almost always the one that involves: being safe, being respectful, reporting something, or asking a supervisor. If you're stuck between two answers, pick the one that does this.
4
It's okay to ask how much time you have
Before starting, it's reasonable to ask: "About how much time do I have for this?" Knowing the time helps you pace yourself without rushing.
5
Remember: this is a "pay attention" test, not a "trick" test
Companies use this quiz to confirm employees understood the videos — not to fail people. If Leo has gone through Modules 2–4 of this app and the Practice Test, he has already seen almost everything that could be asked.
